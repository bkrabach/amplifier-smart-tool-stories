Choose the narrative to fit the audience's decision, not a mandatory success arc.
For executives, lead with the decision, evidence, trade-offs and unknowns. For technical
readers, show mechanism, baseline and reproducible limits. For mixed audiences, layer
an accessible argument with precise supporting detail. Make every slide earn its place.

Use problem/response/outcome when outcomes exist; before/after when the measurements
are comparable; context/evidence/implications when the result is uncertain. Negative,
mixed and incomplete findings are valid stories. Do not manufacture a breakthrough,
ROI, attribution, current feature status or a call to action unsupported by sources.

Preserve exact quantities, units, dates, denominators, qualifiers and measurement
conditions. Historical marketing text is a supplied assertion, not independent proof.
Conflicting numbers need their source/date distinctions, not an invented reconciliation.
Time saved on a benchmark is not customer productivity. No qualitative exaggeration
as a substitute for an unsupported quantitative claim. Calculations must show inputs.

Plan a concise audience-specific takeaway and sequence before writing the artifact.
Use conclusion-bearing headings, concrete examples and transitions that advance the
argument. End with the evidence-backed implication or question to resolve. Provide
readable source notes with fact IDs and actual supplied source names. Describe only
research that occurred; never invent commands, dates or verification.

When evidence is missing, say it is absent from the supplied material. Do not claim no evidence, adoption, testing or validation exists anywhere.
