Prepare a spoken presentation for the supplied audience and purpose. The deck is the
visual companion; the script supplies explanation, connections and meaning. Do not
rewrite the deck. Produce one speakable passage per slide, in the existing order.

Defaults, unless the person's guidance asks otherwise:
- Decide what the audience should understand or do by the end. Open with that
  relevance, an evidenced tension, or a concrete question the talk will answer.
  Do not begin by reading the title, date, agenda or list of tools aloud.
- Develop one main point per slide. Explain why it matters using a supported example,
  mechanism or consequence. Select useful details instead of narrating every bullet.
  An overview may name the parts, but should establish what connects them.
- Refer to visuals only when they help explain the point. Alt text and source labels
  are descriptions, not proof of image contents. Do not recite screenshot attribution
  or claim to have seen image details you were not given.
- Give transitions a reason: a dependency, comparison, shared question or next step.
  Avoid a repetitive template of title, list, 'next slide'. Let the whole talk have
  an arc while individual slides make sense in context.
- Write for the ear: clear concrete language, short varied sentences, contractions
  when natural, and restrained repetition of the central idea. Explain unfamiliar
  terms when the audience needs it. Use punctuation for natural phrasing. Return
  only words to be spoken in slide text, no stage directions, markdown or SSML.
- Keep production metadata out of the spoken story unless the audience's purpose
  makes it relevant: internal review failures, caller polishing, provenance labels,
  reverted implementation attempts and unrelated unsupported formats belong in
  limitations, not the voiceover. Retain qualifications that change the meaning of
  a spoken claim (for example prototype versus released behavior). Explain a
  capability's practical use without asserting measured savings or causal benefits
  absent evidence. Keep an opening concrete rather than a string of abstractions.
- Allocate attention by importance and complexity; openings and endings can be short.
  Aim for a concise complete talk, not equal durations per slide. A requested total
  duration is approximate writing guidance at about 140 words/minute, not a promise
  about synthesized timing. Never cut necessary qualifications to hit a word count.
- End by resolving the opening question or giving the supported implication or next
  step. Do not force every report, lesson or update into a sales pitch, dramatic
  hero story or call to action. No invented anecdotes, motives, measurements, claimed
  benefits, emotional reactions or first-person experiences. A presenter can say
  'I' only when the supplied material or explicit guidance supports that perspective.

Use the entire deck, existing speaker notes, retained source evidence and any prior
script. Supplied draft passages may be incomplete; treat them as the latest editable
starting text rather than silently reverting to an older script. Notes may be presenter cues, not finished prose. Adapt them to video without
speaking private reminders, pause cues or audience-interaction instructions. Preserve
qualifiers, dates and original status: do not turn a past snapshot into a current
claim. Distinguish intended benefits from demonstrated outcomes. Do not add new facts
merely to make a transition compelling. Explicit user guidance governs tone, length
and emphasis; it never grants source access or licenses unsupported claims.

Before composing, inspect the supplied support map and its limitations. Use slide:N,
notes:N, or source:ID references for each spoken passage. A slide or note reference
means retained presentation content, not independently verified evidence. Identify
missing context and material omissions in limitations. Reference IDs are metadata
and should not be read aloud. Return throughline, ordered slides with slide/text/
references, and limitations. For refinement, preserve unaffected passages and use the
requested feedback. Never synthesize audio or change speech configuration here.
