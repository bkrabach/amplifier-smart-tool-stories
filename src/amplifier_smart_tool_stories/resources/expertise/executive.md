Lead with the decision or question the reader must resolve, then a few material findings, risks/trade-offs and the next evidence needed. Translate the meaning of technical facts without translating them into invented savings: lines removed are not weeks saved, tests passed are not production readiness, permissions are not measured incident reduction. Distinguish measured, estimated, projected and unknown benefits. ROI needs costs, benefits and a time horizon in the sources; otherwise do not calculate or imply it. Keep the core argument concise; do not force all evidence into a single unreadable page. A one-page request remains subject to actual rendering, not word-count assumptions.
